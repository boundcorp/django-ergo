# Prioritized Adopt / Learn / Avoid Roadmap

Source basis:
- Competitive analysis attachment `194b43f8bdfd` for Karpathy/Rohit LLM Wiki v2 vs gstack.
- Follow-up checklist attachment `ab7a4dd383b8` that turns that analysis into an implementation-ready spec.

This report converts those two artifacts into an action order:
- what to adopt now,
- what to learn from but defer,
- what to avoid for now,
- and the smallest experiments that can prove the P0 choices in our system.

## P0 Adoption Opportunities

These are the foundation pieces that should move first because they reduce ambiguity, preserve provenance, and keep the system auditable.

1. **[Both] Three-layer memory model: raw evidence -> synthesized pages -> schema/rules**
   - Adopt the split between append-only evidence, editable synthesis pages, and versioned schema/rules.
   - Why now: it is the core architecture that makes the rest of the workflow reviewable and safe.
   - Source tie-in: checklist lines 16-30 and 262-290.

2. **[LLM Wiki v2 + Both] Markdown-first claim-backed wiki pages with visible provenance**
   - Adopt markdown as the primary storage format, plus frontmatter, claims, source refs, status, confidence, and supersession links.
   - Why now: it preserves human-editable knowledge while keeping traceability to evidence.
   - Source tie-in: checklist lines 190-258 and 270-275.

3. **[gstack + Both] Workflow gates with artifact-backed completion**
   - Adopt the intake -> triage -> review -> apply -> verify chain, where every step is blocked unless the required artifact exists and is linked in the log.
   - Why now: this is the minimum discipline needed to prevent “done in chat” from becoming “done in the system.”
   - Source tie-in: checklist lines 294-377.

4. **[Both] Append-only operation log for every change**
   - Adopt a mandatory log entry for ingestion, synthesis, review, apply, archive, and repair actions.
   - Why now: it is the audit spine that lets us reconstruct who changed what and why.
   - Source tie-in: checklist lines 22, 286-290, 342-355, 375-377, and 433-438.

5. **[Both] Explicit trust scopes with deny-by-default read/write policy**
   - Adopt explicit scope on every page/evidence item and no cross-scope promotion without review.
   - Start with the narrowest scope that matters for the product; repo or project scope is the safest starting point.
   - Source tie-in: checklist lines 27-29, 154-166, and 390-396.

6. **[LLM Wiki v2] Small fixed taxonomy plus lifecycle states**
   - Adopt a minimal fixed type set and explicit lifecycle states instead of unconstrained labels.
   - Why now: it gives tooling and review something stable to enforce without forcing an overly general graph model.
   - Source tie-in: checklist lines 34-45, 51-75, and 98-110.

## P1 Adoption Opportunities

These should follow once the P0 slices are proven in our system.

1. **[LLM Wiki v2] Typed relation vocabulary instead of free-form graph edges**
   - Keep relationships small and typed, but defer richer graph traversal until the typed model proves useful.

2. **[LLM Wiki v2] BM25-only baseline before embeddings**
   - Start with markdown + grep/BM25 style retrieval and only add embeddings after measured miss cases show a real gap.

3. **[gstack] Human approval for semantic changes, not just mechanical ones**
   - Keep the review gate explicit for deletes, downgrades, contradictions, and other semantic edits.

4. **[Both] Crystallization / session-end summaries as first-class artifacts**
   - Use them as the handoff between raw intake and synthesized pages, but do not over-engineer them before the core log/provenance path works.

5. **[Both] Versioned schema and lint rules**
   - Add schema validation and failure classes after the first page/log formats are stable enough to make the rules meaningful.

## P2 Adoption Opportunities

These are useful later, but they are not the right first bets.

1. **[LLM Wiki v2] Embeddings + graph traversal search**
   - High potential, but it should be justified by measured retrieval misses rather than adopted by default.

2. **[LLM Wiki v2] Expanded taxonomy or custom type extension**
   - Useful if the core types become restrictive, but premature flexibility makes the system harder to lint and reason about.

3. **[Both] Broader trust scopes beyond repo/project/ticket**
   - User-private or client-private scopes should wait until the access model is proven and the product need is explicit.

4. **[gstack] Automated repair / event-driven auto-ingest**
   - Valuable only after the schema, trust, and review gates are stable.

5. **[Both] Browser/cookie automation**
   - Keep it out of v1 unless there is a separate, explicit security review and a real workflow requirement.

## Learn But Do Not Adopt Yet

These are the strongest ideas to study from the reference projects, but we should not install them wholesale yet.

1. **[LLM Wiki v2] The “knowledge substrate” idea**
   - Learn how the separation between source evidence and synthesized knowledge keeps a wiki useful over time.
   - Do not adopt the full implementation surface yet, especially graph-heavy or search-heavy parts that are not justified by usage.

2. **[LLM Wiki v2] The claim-centric authoring model**
   - Learn from the idea that pages should be made of checkable claims with provenance.
   - Do not turn this into a complicated schema until we know which claim fields are actually used in review.

3. **[gstack] Role separation and workflow discipline**
   - Learn from the explicit handoff culture, review gates, and artifact requirement.
   - Do not copy a large orchestration surface or assume every step needs automation.

4. **[gstack] “Done” means backed by artifacts**
   - Learn the operational norm that completion requires evidence.
   - Do not adopt opaque automation that hides the evidence path.

5. **[Both] Richer automation only after the manual path is stable**
   - Learn from both projects that the workflow is the product, not just the data model.
   - Do not jump straight to auto-ingest, auto-repair, or browser-driven actions.

## Avoid For Now

These carry a worse risk/reward profile than the alternatives above.

1. **[LLM Wiki v2] Fully flexible page labels**
   - Why avoid: it undermines linting, navigation, and predictable tooling.
   - Tradeoff: it feels easier initially but creates schema drift and makes review harder.

2. **[LLM Wiki v2] Numeric confidence scoring without calibration**
   - Why avoid: it creates false precision unless we already have data to calibrate it.
   - Tradeoff: reviewers may trust the number more than the evidence.

3. **[LLM Wiki v2] Graph traversal as a day-one dependency**
   - Why avoid: it adds complexity before the smaller typed model has proven value.
   - Tradeoff: the system can become harder to explain and debug.

4. **[gstack] Event-driven auto-ingest and auto-repair**
   - Why avoid: hidden automation can mutate knowledge without enough human review.
   - Tradeoff: higher throughput, but much weaker auditability and easier failure masking.

5. **[gstack] Persistent browser daemon with imported auth state**
   - Why avoid: it increases secret exposure and creates a new attack surface.
   - Tradeoff: convenience, but at a materially higher security and ops cost.

6. **[Both] Silent hard delete or silent overwrite of evidence**
   - Why avoid: it breaks provenance and makes later audit impossible.
   - Tradeoff: more storage retention, but much safer reconstruction and review.

7. **[Both] “Done” without a linked artifact**
   - Why avoid: it defeats the review model and makes workflow status unreliable.
   - Tradeoff: slightly more process overhead, but much better correctness.

## P0 Experiments

These are the smallest tests that can tell us whether the P0 choices work in our system.

| P0 item | Smallest experiment | What to measure | Expected outcome | How to know it worked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Three-layer memory model | Ingest a small batch of representative evidence into `evidence/`, synthesize 5-10 pages into `wiki/`, and record the schema version used. | Time to go from source to page, number of claims per page, and whether every claim has a source ref. | We can clearly separate raw evidence from synthesized pages without losing traceability. | A reviewer can trace each claim back to a source in under 5 minutes, and there are no active claims without source refs. |
| Markdown-first claim-backed pages | Build one decision page and one concept page using the proposed frontmatter and claim format. | Frontmatter completeness, claim provenance coverage, and edit/supersession behavior. | The format is readable, editable, and preserves provenance. | The page can be updated without losing history, and supersession links remain intact after an edit. |
| Workflow gates with artifact-backed completion | Run one full intake -> triage -> review -> apply -> verify cycle on a synthetic ticket. | Whether each step produces the required artifact and whether the log links to it. | The workflow blocks completion until the artifact exists. | A step cannot be marked done without the artifact; the log contains actor, timestamp, reason, source refs, and touched pages. |
| Append-only operation log | Add writes, a review decision, and an archive action to a log-only sandbox. | Completeness of each log entry and whether the log is append-only. | Every action is reconstructable from the log. | We can replay the sequence from the log alone, and no entry needs in-place mutation to remain accurate. |
| Explicit trust scopes | Run one positive and one negative access test for the chosen initial scope boundary. | Whether denied reads/writes are actually blocked and whether the denial is logged. | Deny-by-default works for out-of-scope reads/writes. | The negative test fails closed, the allowed test passes, and the access decision is visible in the log. |
| Small fixed taxonomy and lifecycle states | Classify a small corpus of pages into the proposed types and states. | Number of ambiguous classifications and number of pages that need custom type workarounds. | The fixed taxonomy is sufficient for a first pass. | Fewer than a small minority of pages need exceptions, and the reviewer can classify the rest consistently. |

## Dependencies, Assumptions, and Open Questions

### Dependencies and assumptions

- We are assuming the first useful version stays small and manually reviewable.
- We are assuming markdown remains the authoring format for v1.
- We are assuming a ticket/project scope can be enforced without importing browser auth state.
- We are assuming the system will keep raw evidence append-only and never rewrite it in place.
- We are assuming the ticketing/workflow layer can attach or link artifacts reliably.

### Open questions

1. What is the first scope boundary we should enforce: repo, project, ticket, or user-private?
2. Which page/entity types are day-one mandatory, and which should wait?
3. What retention window do we want for raw evidence and session artifacts?
4. Which change classes require human approval: all semantic changes, or only deletes/downgrades/contradictions?
5. Should search stay BM25-only for v1, or is there already a measured recall gap that justifies embeddings?
6. Do we want graph edges in frontmatter in v1, or should graph materialization wait?
7. Is browser automation part of this product now, or a separate security-reviewed product decision?
8. What exact verification artifacts make a workflow step “done” in our system?

## Recommendation

If we only execute a few things immediately, do these in order:

1. Lock the memory model and frontmatter/claim shape.
2. Enforce artifact-backed workflow completion and append-only logs.
3. Add explicit scope controls.
4. Keep search, graphing, and browser automation out of the first pass unless a measured need appears.
